# Stage 1 — Lifted policy semantic core (results)

> Companion to [01_plan.md](01_plan.md). Upstream draft: [00_draft_lifted_policy_az.md](../00_draft_lifted_policy_az.md).

## §0 Introduction

This stage built the **lifted decision-list DSL** and an **online unification interpreter** that together form the dispatch primitive a future MCTS / `LeafEvaluator` will call once per env step. Both modules sit under [src/alphazeropp/synthesis/](../../../../src/alphazeropp/synthesis/) and are intentionally domain-agnostic — they know nothing about Doors, Gripper, or any specific env. To exercise them in isolation we also wrote a minimal relational env, [src/alphazeropp/instances/gripper_lite/](../../../../src/alphazeropp/instances/gripper_lite/), and a hand-written four-rule policy that solves Gripper-lite for any number of balls.

The load-bearing claim of the stage:

> A single typed lifted policy with four rules, dispatched by a generic online-unification interpreter, solves Gripper-lite for B = 1, 2, 3 with identical rule-firing structure across instance sizes and identical structure under object renaming.

That claim is proved end-to-end by [tests/test_gripper_lite_policy.py](../../../../tests/test_gripper_lite_policy.py) and the rollout traces below.

**In scope for Stage 1.** Pure semantic core: a typed lifted DSL with state/goal literals, safe negation, lex-min unification, and a tiny relational env. Hand-written policies only.

**Out of scope for Stage 1.** Grammar productions, MCTS coupling, learning, multi-domain transfer, performance characterization. Those are Stage 2 and beyond.

![Gripper-lite domain schematic](../figures/gripper_lite_domain.png)

> *Gripper-lite domain. Two rooms, B balls (here B = 2), a robot with one gripper, hand empty. Goal (hollow rings in `room_b`): every ball ends up in `room_b`. Actions: `move(room, room)`, `pick(ball, room)`, `drop(ball, room)`.*

## §0.5 Where Doors sits

Gripper-lite is intentionally tiny. The project's primary instance is **Doors** — a multi-room navigation domain with keys, doors, and locking dependencies — which already has end-to-end AlphaZero/MCTS coupling, a flat-vector gymnasium wrapper ([`DoorsPDDLLiteEnv`](../../../../src/alphazeropp/instances/doors/doors_pddl_lite.py)), and **five stacked DSL layers** for grammar-based synthesis ([Surface](../../../../src/alphazeropp/instances/doors/dsl/surface_dsl.py), [Stage](../../../../src/alphazeropp/instances/doors/dsl/stage_dsl.py), [Lifted](../../../../src/alphazeropp/instances/doors/dsl/lifted_typed_dsl.py), [Reactive-Sketch](../../../../src/alphazeropp/instances/doors/dsl/reactive_sketch_dsl.py), [Reactive-Typed](../../../../src/alphazeropp/instances/doors/dsl/reactive_typed_grammar.py)). Doors is where the production pipeline lives: configurable size (D rooms, M locations, K keys), action masking, neural value/policy nets, and ablation infrastructure (see [`doors/eval_ablations.py`](../../../../src/alphazeropp/instances/doors/eval_ablations.py) and [`doors/network.py`](../../../../src/alphazeropp/instances/doors/network.py)).

Gripper-lite occupies the opposite end of the complexity axis — a minimal *semantic-core* probe used here only to validate the new domain-agnostic lifted DSL + online-unification interpreter under `src/alphazeropp/synthesis/` without taking on Doors' integration surface. Doors carries the question *"can we synthesize good policies under MCTS?"*; Gripper-lite asks the upstream question *"does the lifted-policy dispatch primitive even work?"* Stage 2 will close the gap by wiring `gripper_lite` into the existing [`DerivationGame`](../../../../src/alphazeropp/synthesis/derivation_game.py), at which point Gripper-lite graduates to a second production domain alongside Doors.

## §2 The four hand-written rules

[src/alphazeropp/instances/gripper_lite/policies.py](../../../../src/alphazeropp/instances/gripper_lite/policies.py):

| # | Body (state ∧ goal literals) | Action | Intuition |
|---|---|---|---|
| ρ₁ | `carrying(?b) ∧ at_robot(?r) ∧ Goal[at_ball(?b, ?r)]` | `drop(?b, ?r)` | I'm carrying a ball and standing where it needs to be. Drop it. |
| ρ₂ | `carrying(?b) ∧ at_robot(?from) ∧ Goal[at_ball(?b, ?to)]` | `move(?from, ?to)` | Carrying a ball but in the wrong room. Walk it there. |
| ρ₃ | `at_ball(?b, ?r) ∧ at_robot(?r) ∧ handempty() ∧ ¬Goal[at_ball(?b, ?r)]` | `pick(?b, ?r)` | I'm next to a misplaced ball with a free hand. Pick it up. |
| ρ₄ | `at_robot(?from) ∧ at_ball(?b, ?to) ∧ handempty() ∧ ¬Goal[at_ball(?b, ?to)]` | `move(?from, ?to)` | Hand free, a misplaced ball exists elsewhere. Walk to it. |

The order matters — the interpreter fires the **first** rule whose body admits a binding, breaking ties lex-min over the variables. ρ₁ and ρ₂ deal with the "ball in hand" case; ρ₃ and ρ₄ deal with the "ball on floor" case.

### Policy in math form

$$
\boxed{\;\rho_1:\quad \text{carrying}(?b)\;\wedge\;\text{at\_robot}(?r)\;\wedge\;\text{Goal}\,\text{at\_ball}(?b,?r) \;\Longrightarrow\; \text{drop}(?b,?r)\;}
$$

$$
\boxed{\;\rho_2:\quad \text{carrying}(?b)\;\wedge\;\text{at\_robot}(?\textit{from})\;\wedge\;\text{Goal}\,\text{at\_ball}(?b,?\textit{to}) \;\Longrightarrow\; \text{move}(?\textit{from},?\textit{to})\;}
$$

$$
\boxed{\;\rho_3:\quad \text{at\_ball}(?b,?r)\;\wedge\;\text{at\_robot}(?r)\;\wedge\;\text{handempty}()\;\wedge\;\neg\,\text{Goal}\,\text{at\_ball}(?b,?r) \;\Longrightarrow\; \text{pick}(?b,?r)\;}
$$

$$
\boxed{\;\rho_4:\quad \text{at\_robot}(?\textit{from})\;\wedge\;\text{at\_ball}(?b,?\textit{to})\;\wedge\;\text{handempty}()\;\wedge\;\neg\,\text{Goal}\,\text{at\_ball}(?b,?\textit{to}) \;\Longrightarrow\; \text{move}(?\textit{from},?\textit{to})\;}
$$

### Worked binding example (B=2, initial state)

$$
s_0 \;=\; \bigl\{\,\text{at\_robot}(\text{room\_a}),\; \text{handempty}(),\; \text{at\_ball}(\text{ball\_0},\text{room\_a}),\; \text{at\_ball}(\text{ball\_1},\text{room\_a})\,\bigr\}
$$

$$
g \;=\; \bigl\{\,\text{at\_ball}(\text{ball\_0},\text{room\_b}),\; \text{at\_ball}(\text{ball\_1},\text{room\_b})\,\bigr\}
$$

- ρ₁ requires `carrying(?b)` — fails, nothing carried.
- ρ₂ requires `carrying(?b)` — fails.
- ρ₃ requires `at_ball(?b, ?r) ∧ at_robot(?r) ∧ handempty()`. After positive matching, the candidate `(b, r)` pairs are `{(ball_0, room_a), (ball_1, room_a)}` (the only ones with `at_robot(?r)`). `¬Goal[at_ball(?b, ?r)]` filters out goal pairs (`(*, room_b)`) but both candidates are room_a, so both survive. Lex-min over `(?b, ?r)` selects `θ = {?b = ball_0, ?r = room_a}` → `pick(ball_0, room_a)`. ρ₃ fires.

This is exactly the trace shown in §3 for B=2 step 0.

The closed-form step count is **4B − 1**: each ball costs `pick → move → drop` (3 steps) plus a `move` back to room_a (1 step) — except the final ball, which doesn't need the trailing return trip.

The rule-firing pattern is identical across B = 1, 2, 3 — the **same four rules** in the **same canonical order** (ρ₃, ρ₂, ρ₁, [ρ₄, ρ₃, ρ₂, ρ₁]\*) generate every solution. That is precisely the property a lifted policy is supposed to have.

## §4 Object-renaming: name-agnostic matching (not full permutation equivariance)

The property under test is that **`find_bindings` reads only the relational
structure of `(state, goal)`, not the raw object names** — the lifted dispatch
primitive is *name-agnostic*. The deterministic tie-break used by
`interpret` is, however, lexicographic over **raw object names** ([00 §5
"Renaming caveat"](../00_draft_lifted_policy_az.md#5-the-online-unification-interpreter)),
so the equivariance equation

$$
\text{rollout}\bigl(\sigma(s_0),\,\sigma(g)\bigr) \;=\; \sigma\bigl(\text{rollout}(s_0,\,g)\bigr)
$$

holds **only when the renaming $\sigma$ preserves the induced object order**
(or on states with no contested tie). The σ used in this test —
$\{\text{ball\_0} \mapsto \text{foo},\; \text{room\_a} \mapsto \text{left\_room}\}$,
identity elsewhere — is order-preserving on the ball axis ($B=1$, single ball)
and on the rooms with no contested tie, so the equation holds; the test
therefore demonstrates **name-agnostic matching**, not full permutation
equivariance over arbitrary $\sigma$. Achieving the latter would require
either transporting the object order alongside σ or canonicalising the order
independently of raw names; both are out of scope for Stage 1.

Concretely we apply $\sigma = \{\text{ball\_0} \mapsto \text{foo},\; \text{room\_a} \mapsto \text{left\_room}\}$ (identity elsewhere). The same policy, the same interpreter, applied to the renamed instance produces the renamed rollout:

| step | rule | grounded action (canonical) | grounded action (renamed) |
|---:|---:|---|---|
| 0 | ρ₃ | `pick(ball_0, room_a)` | `pick(foo, left_room)` |
| 1 | ρ₂ | `move(room_a, room_b)` | `move(left_room, room_b)` |
| 2 | ρ₁ | `drop(ball_0, room_b)` | `drop(foo, room_b)` |

The action schemas and rule indices match step-for-step; only the bound objects change. [tests/test_gripper_lite_policy.py::test_object_renaming_preserves_behavior](../../../../tests/test_gripper_lite_policy.py) asserts this by composing the canonical-to-renamed map and checking each `(schema, mapped_args, rule_index)` triple equals its renamed counterpart. (The test's symbol name `…preserves_behavior` is a slight overstatement: it preserves behaviour for *this* σ, which is order-preserving — see the caveat above. Renaming the symbol is left as a tidy-up; the docs are the load-bearing record.)

This is the visual justification of Stage 0 §1's claim — *the same lifted rules generalize because the dispatch primitive matches relational structure, not raw object names*.

## §7 What's next

Stage 2 will replace `hand_policy()` with a **CFG-driven production emitter** that the existing [`DerivationGame`](../../../../src/alphazeropp/synthesis/derivation_game.py) can drive. The signature `interpret(policy, state, goal, objects, legal_actions, *, trace=False) -> GroundAction | None` defined here is the dispatch primitive that the LeafEvaluator (Stage 3) will call once per env step. The Gripper-lite env will graduate from "Stage-1 test rig" to "second domain alongside Doors" the moment a leaf evaluator wires it up.
