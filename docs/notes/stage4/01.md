# Stage 1 (rev. 1) — Lifted semantic core on two relational contracts (results)

## §0 Introduction

This stage built (v0) and then extended (rev. 1) the **lifted decision-list DSL** + an **online-unification
interpreter** that together form the dispatch primitive a future grammar / MCTS / `LeafEvaluator` will call once per env
step. Both modules — [lifted_dsl.py](../../../src/alphazeropp/synthesis/lifted_dsl.py),
[lifted_interpreter.py](../../../src/alphazeropp/synthesis/lifted_interpreter.py) — are domain-agnostic. v0 exercised
them on a minimal Gripper-lite relational env; rev. 1 wires a **second** domain — a relational adapter over the project's
primary `DoorsPDDLLiteEnv` — and shows the *same* `interpret(...)` dispatches a hand-written policy there too.

The load-bearing claim of the stage:

> A single generic online-unification interpreter dispatches typed lifted policies over **two structurally different
> relational env contracts** — Gripper-lite and a relational adapter over Doors PDDL — with the rule-firing structure
> invariant across instance sizes and under object renaming. A 4-rule hand policy solves Gripper-lite for $B \in
> \{1,2,3\}$; a 3-rule hand policy solves the two shipped Doors layouts D2 and D3.

That claim is proved end-to-end by `tests/test_gripper_lite_policy.py`, `tests/test_doors_pddl_lifted_contract.py`,
`tests/test_doors_pddl_lifted_interpreter.py`, and the rollout traces below.

**In scope for Stage 1.** Pure semantic core: a typed lifted DSL (state/goal literals, safe negation, lex-min
unification), an online-unification interpreter, and two relational envs satisfying one shared contract. Hand-written
policies only.

**Out of scope for Stage 1.** Grammar productions, derivation game, MCTS, learned policy/value net, leaf-evaluator
wiring, multi-domain *generalization*, performance characterization. Those are Stage 2 and beyond. *No grammar and no
MCTS appear anywhere in this stage's deliverables.*

![Gripper-lite domain schematic](figures/gripper_lite_domain.png)

> *Gripper-lite domain. Two rooms, $B$ balls (here $B=2$), a robot with one gripper, hand empty. Goal (hollow rings in
> `room_b`): every ball ends up in `room_b`. Actions: `move(room, room)`, `pick(ball, room)`, `drop(ball, room)`.*

## §2 Gripper-lite — the four hand-written rules

[src/alphazeropp/instances/gripper_lite/policies.py](../../../src/alphazeropp/instances/gripper_lite/policies.py):

| # | Body (state ∧ goal literals) | Action | Intuition |
|---|---|---|---|
| ρ₁ | `carrying(?b) ∧ at_robot(?r) ∧ Goal[at_ball(?b, ?r)]` | `drop(?b, ?r)` | Carrying a ball, standing where it needs to be. Drop it. |
| ρ₂ | `carrying(?b) ∧ at_robot(?from) ∧ Goal[at_ball(?b, ?to)]` | `move(?from, ?to)` | Carrying a ball but in the wrong room. Walk it there. |
| ρ₃ | `at_ball(?b, ?r) ∧ at_robot(?r) ∧ handempty() ∧ ¬Goal[at_ball(?b, ?r)]` | `pick(?b, ?r)` | Next to a misplaced ball, free hand. Pick it up. |
| ρ₄ | `at_robot(?from) ∧ at_ball(?b, ?to) ∧ handempty() ∧ ¬Goal[at_ball(?b, ?to)]` | `move(?from, ?to)` | Hand free, a misplaced ball exists elsewhere. Walk to it. |

$$
\boxed{\;\rho_1:\ \mathrm{carrying}(?b)\wedge\mathrm{at\_robot}(?r)\wedge\mathrm{Goal}\,\mathrm{at\_ball}(?b,?r)\;\Rightarrow\;\mathrm{drop}(?b,?r)\;}
$$
$$
\boxed{\;\rho_2:\ \mathrm{carrying}(?b)\wedge\mathrm{at\_robot}(?\textit{from})\wedge\mathrm{Goal}\,\mathrm{at\_ball}(?b,?\textit{to})\;\Rightarrow\;\mathrm{move}(?\textit{from},?\textit{to})\;}
$$
$$
\boxed{\;\rho_3:\ \mathrm{at\_ball}(?b,?r)\wedge\mathrm{at\_robot}(?r)\wedge\mathrm{handempty}()\wedge\neg\,\mathrm{Goal}\,\mathrm{at\_ball}(?b,?r)\;\Rightarrow\;\mathrm{pick}(?b,?r)\;}
$$
$$
\boxed{\;\rho_4:\ \mathrm{at\_robot}(?\textit{from})\wedge\mathrm{at\_ball}(?b,?\textit{to})\wedge\mathrm{handempty}()\wedge\neg\,\mathrm{Goal}\,\mathrm{at\_ball}(?b,?\textit{to})\;\Rightarrow\;\mathrm{move}(?\textit{from},?\textit{to})\;}
$$

The order matters — the interpreter fires the **first** rule whose body admits a binding, breaking ties lex-min over the
variables. ρ₂ uses a *body-local variable* `?b` (it occurs in `carrying(?b)` and `Goal[at_ball(?b,?to)]` but is not a
`move` argument).

### Worked binding example (B=2, initial state)

$$
s_0 = \{\,\mathrm{at\_robot}(\mathrm{room\_a}),\;\mathrm{handempty}(),\;\mathrm{at\_ball}(\mathrm{ball\_0},\mathrm{room\_a}),\;\mathrm{at\_ball}(\mathrm{ball\_1},\mathrm{room\_a})\,\},\qquad
g = \{\,\mathrm{at\_ball}(\mathrm{ball\_0},\mathrm{room\_b}),\;\mathrm{at\_ball}(\mathrm{ball\_1},\mathrm{room\_b})\,\}
$$

ρ₁/ρ₂ require `carrying(?b)` — fail (nothing carried). ρ₃ requires `at_ball(?b,?r) ∧ at_robot(?r) ∧ handempty()`; the
candidate `(?b,?r)` pairs are `{(ball_0,room_a),(ball_1,room_a)}`, and `¬Goal[at_ball(?b,?r)]` keeps both (neither is a
`(*, room_b)` goal pair). Lex-min over `(?b,?r)` selects `θ = {?b=ball_0, ?r=room_a}` → `pick(ball_0, room_a)`. ρ₃
fires — exactly step 0 of the B=2 rollout below.

(ρ₃, ρ₂, ρ₁, [ρ₄, ρ₃, ρ₂, ρ₁]\*). That is precisely the property a lifted policy is supposed to have.

## §4 Gripper-lite — object-renaming: name-agnostic matching (not full permutation equivariance)

`find_bindings` reads only the relational structure of `(state, goal)`, not the raw object names. The deterministic
tie-break used by `interpret` is, however, lexicographic over **raw object names** ([01_draft_lifted_policy_az.md §7,
"Renaming caveat"](01_draft_lifted_policy_az.md#7-the-online-unification-interpreter)), so

$$
\mathrm{rollout}\bigl(\sigma(s_0),\,\sigma(g)\bigr) \;=\; \sigma\bigl(\mathrm{rollout}(s_0,\,g)\bigr)
$$

holds **only when $\sigma$ preserves the induced object order** (or on states with no contested tie). The σ used in the
test — $\{\mathrm{ball\_0}\mapsto\mathrm{foo},\;\mathrm{room\_a}\mapsto\mathrm{left\_room}\}$ (identity elsewhere) — is
order-preserving, so the equation holds; the test therefore demonstrates **name-agnostic matching**, not arbitrary
permutation equivariance.

| step | rule | grounded action (canonical) | grounded action (renamed) |
|---:|---:|---|---|
| 0 | ρ₃ | `pick(ball_0, room_a)` | `pick(foo, left_room)` |
| 1 | ρ₂ | `move(room_a, room_b)` | `move(left_room, room_b)` |
| 2 | ρ₁ | `drop(ball_0, room_b)` | `drop(foo, room_b)` |

Action schemas and rule indices match step-for-step; only the bound objects change.
[tests/test_gripper_lite_policy.py::test_object_renaming_preserves_behavior](../../../tests/test_gripper_lite_policy.py)
asserts this.

## §5 Doors PDDL — the relational adapter

`DoorsPDDLLiteEnv` ([doors/doors_pddl_lite.py](../../../src/alphazeropp/instances/doors/doors_pddl_lite.py)) is a
gymnasium flat-vector env (`spaces.Box` obs, `spaces.Discrete` int actions, integer object ids), wired into the AlphaZero
*benchmark* harness — it has no relational view. Stage 1 rev. 1 adds `DoorsPDDLLiteRelationalEnv`
([doors/doors_pddl_lifted.py](../../../src/alphazeropp/instances/doors/doors_pddl_lifted.py)): a thin wrapper holding a
`DoorsPDDLLiteEnv` (`.base`; the gym interface untouched and verified by
`test_doors_pddl_lifted_contract.py::test_gym_interface_untouched`) and exposing the *same* lifted env contract
Gripper-lite exposes. The underlying env keeps its integer ids; the adapter maps them to stable strings
(`loc_l` / `room_r` / `key_k`; the prefixes are configurable via `name_fmt`, used by the renaming test).

**Vocabulary** (refines the orientation doc §4.2 sketch — which was flagged "not yet implemented" and listed only the
three fluents — by adding three *static* relations so meaningful Doors rules are expressible):

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{Doors}} &= \{\textit{room},\,\textit{location},\,\textit{key}\}\\
\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{fluent}}_{\mathrm{Doors}} &= \{\,\mathrm{at\_loc}(\textit{location}),\;\mathrm{unlocked}(\textit{room}),\;\mathrm{key\_avail}(\textit{key})\,\}\\
\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{static}}_{\mathrm{Doors}} &= \{\,\mathrm{loc\_in\_room}(\textit{location},\textit{room}),\;\mathrm{key\_at}(\textit{key},\textit{location}),\;\mathrm{key\_unlocks}(\textit{key},\textit{room})\,\}\\
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{Doors}} &= \{\,\mathrm{move\_to}(\textit{location}),\;\mathrm{pick}(\textit{key}),\;\mathrm{noop}()\,\}\\
G_{\mathrm{Doors}} &= \{\,\mathrm{at\_loc}(\textit{goal location})\,\}\qquad(\text{so }\mathrm{goal\_predicate\_names} = (\mathrm{at\_loc},))
\end{aligned}}
$$

The fluents come from `base._state` (via the precomputed offsets `base.M` / `base._unlocked_offset` /
`base._key_offset`); the statics from `base.loc_room` / `base.key_loc` / `base.key_unlocks` — in PDDL terms the static
predicates of the problem. The `DomainSignature` for the domain is `doors_pddl_signature()` in
[lifted_grammar.py](../../../src/alphazeropp/synthesis/lifted_grammar.py) (next to `gripper_lite_signature()`).
`legal_actions()` is the relational mirror of `base.action_masks("precondition")` — `move_to(loc_l)` iff
`unlocked[loc_room[l]]`, `pick(key_k)` iff the agent is at `key_loc[k]` and `key_available[k]`, `noop()` always —
verified equal to the decoded mask by `test_legal_actions_mirrors_base_action_masks`, and the dynamics are verified
identical to the underlying env by `test_step_parity_with_base_env`.

### D2 reset state (the contract, concretely)

`DoorsPDDLLiteRelationalEnv.make_d2()` — D=2 rooms, M=4 locations, K=1 key; key at `loc_1` unlocks `room_1`; start
`loc_0`, goal `loc_3`; horizon 15:

```
get_objects_by_type() = {"room": ("room_0","room_1"),
                         "location": ("loc_0","loc_1","loc_2","loc_3"),
                         "key": ("key_0",)}
get_goal_atoms()["at_loc"]  = {("loc_3",)}            # all other predicates: empty set

get_state_atoms() =
  at_loc      = {("loc_0",)}                          # exactly one
  unlocked    = {("room_0",)}                         # room_1 still locked
  key_avail   = {("key_0",)}
  loc_in_room = {("loc_0","room_0"), ("loc_1","room_0"), ("loc_2","room_1"), ("loc_3","room_1")}   # static
  key_at      = {("key_0","loc_1")}                   # static
  key_unlocks = {("key_0","room_1")}                  # static

legal_actions() = { move_to(loc_0), move_to(loc_1), noop() }   # room_1 locked, not standing on a key

D2 layout:    room_0 [unlocked]            room_1 [locked]
              loc_0  loc_1(key_0)   --door-->   loc_2   loc_3(GOAL)
              ^agent
```

## §6 Doors PDDL — rollout traces (the 3-rule hand policy)

`doors_hand_policy()` ([doors/doors_pddl_policies.py](../../../src/alphazeropp/instances/doors/doors_pddl_policies.py)),
first-applicable order:

| # | Body | Action | Intuition |
|---|---|---|---|
| ρ₁ | `Goal[at_loc(?l)] ∧ unlocked(?r) ∧ loc_in_room(?l, ?r)` | `move_to(?l)` | Goal location's room is reachable. Walk there. |
| ρ₂ | `at_loc(?l) ∧ key_at(?k, ?l) ∧ key_avail(?k)` | `pick(?k)` | Standing on an available key. Pick it (unlocks a room). |
| ρ₃ | `key_at(?k, ?l) ∧ key_avail(?k) ∧ loc_in_room(?l, ?r) ∧ unlocked(?r)` | `move_to(?l)` | Otherwise: walk to a reachable available key. |

$$
\boxed{\;\rho_1:\ \mathrm{Goal}[\mathrm{at\_loc}(?l)]\wedge\mathrm{unlocked}(?r)\wedge\mathrm{loc\_in\_room}(?l,?r)\;\Rightarrow\;\mathrm{move\_to}(?l)\;}
$$
$$
\boxed{\;\rho_2:\ \mathrm{at\_loc}(?l)\wedge\mathrm{key\_at}(?k,?l)\wedge\mathrm{key\_avail}(?k)\;\Rightarrow\;\mathrm{pick}(?k)\;}
$$
$$
\boxed{\;\rho_3:\ \mathrm{key\_at}(?k,?l)\wedge\mathrm{key\_avail}(?k)\wedge\mathrm{loc\_in\_room}(?l,?r)\wedge\mathrm{unlocked}(?r)\;\Rightarrow\;\mathrm{move\_to}(?l)\;}
$$

No negation appears, so safe-negation is vacuously satisfied. Traces captured from `interpret(..., trace=True)` by
`scripts/run/run_lifted_doors_pddl_smoke.py` (and asserted by `test_doors_pddl_lifted_interpreter.py`):

### D2 (3 steps; goal `loc_3`)

| step | rule | grounded action | θ | why (first-applicable) |
|---:|---:|---|---|---|
| 0 | ρ₃ (idx 2) | `move_to(loc_1)` | `?k=key_0, ?l=loc_1, ?r=room_0` | ρ₁ fails — `loc_in_room(loc_3, room_0)` ∉ state (loc_3 ∈ room_1, locked). ρ₂ fails — no `key_at(?k, loc_0)`. ρ₃ — key_0 at loc_1 ∈ room_0 (unlocked). |
| 1 | ρ₂ (idx 1) | `pick(key_0)` | `?k=key_0, ?l=loc_1` | now `at_loc(loc_1) ∧ key_at(key_0, loc_1) ∧ key_avail(key_0)`. Picking unlocks `room_1`. |
| 2 | ρ₁ (idx 0) | `move_to(loc_3)` | `?l=loc_3, ?r=room_1` | `Goal[at_loc(loc_3)] ∧ unlocked(room_1) ∧ loc_in_room(loc_3, room_1)`. → goal. **solved.** |

### D3 (5 steps; D=3, M=6, K=2; goal `loc_5`)

| step | rule | grounded action | θ |
|---:|---:|---|---|
| 0 | ρ₃ (idx 2) | `move_to(loc_1)` | `?k=key_0, ?l=loc_1, ?r=room_0` |
| 1 | ρ₂ (idx 1) | `pick(key_0)` | `?k=key_0, ?l=loc_1` |
| 2 | ρ₃ (idx 2) | `move_to(loc_2)` | `?k=key_1, ?l=loc_2, ?r=room_1` |
| 3 | ρ₂ (idx 1) | `pick(key_1)` | `?k=key_1, ?l=loc_2` |
| 4 | ρ₁ (idx 0) | `move_to(loc_5)` | `?l=loc_5, ?r=room_2` |

The same three rules, the same canonical order ([ρ₃, ρ₂]\* then ρ₁), at both instance sizes. The step 0 ρ₃ binding is
unambiguous — of the two `key_at(?k,?l) ∧ key_avail(?k) ∧ loc_in_room(?l,?r)` candidates only the `key_0` row survives
`unlocked(?r)` (room_1 still locked) — and no contested lex-min tie arises anywhere in either rollout.

> Observation: with $K$ keys arranged in a chain, this policy solves the chained-unlock layout in $2K{+}1$ steps ($K$
> `move_to` + $K$ `pick` + 1 final `move_to`): D2 → 3, D3 → 5. This is a fact about *this hand policy* on *these shipped
> layouts* — **not** a generalization claim about synthesized policies, and **not** an MCTS result. Stage 1 has no
> grammar and no search.

### Doors object renaming

`test_doors_object_renaming_preserves_structure` rebuilds the D2 adapter with `name_fmt = {"location":"site_{}",
"room":"area_{}", "key":"tool_{}"}` (an order-preserving σ: same integer ids, different prefixes) and re-runs
`doors_hand_policy()`:

| step | rule | grounded action (canonical) | grounded action (renamed) |
|---:|---:|---|---|
| 0 | ρ₃ | `move_to(loc_1)` | `move_to(site_1)` |
| 1 | ρ₂ | `pick(key_0)` | `pick(tool_0)` |
| 2 | ρ₁ | `move_to(loc_3)` | `move_to(site_3)` |

Schemas and rule indices match step-for-step; only the bound objects change — the same name-agnostic-matching property
as Gripper-lite §4 (and the same caveat: order-preserving σ, not arbitrary permutations).

## §9 Limitations

- Hand-written policies only — Stage 1 does not show any search discovers either policy. (No grammar, no MCTS, no
  learned net, no leaf-evaluator wiring exists yet.)
- The Doors D2/D3 solves (and the $2K{+}1$-step observation) are *dispatch-primitive demonstrations on fixed shipped
  layouts*, not generalization to larger / structurally-different Doors instances. No Doors policy was *synthesized*.
- Object-renaming invariance holds for *order-preserving* relabelings (the lex-min tie-break is over raw object names);
  arbitrary permutation equivariance is not provided (orientation §7 caveat). For Doors the shipped layouts have
  single-digit ids and no contested tie, so determinism is unaffected; zero-padding the names would be the general fix.
- The `pick` schema has *different arities* in the two domains — `pick(ball, room)` for Gripper-lite, `pick(key)` for
  Doors — which is fine (a policy is written against one domain's signature; the two are never co-resident).
- The Doors adapter exposes `noop()`, but `doors_hand_policy()` never needs it; it is present for completeness of the
  action vocabulary.
- The Doors adapter reads `base._state` and the precomputed offsets directly (rather than re-deriving them from
  `feature_spec`); equivalent, and unaffected by any change to `DoorsPDDLLiteEnv` that keeps those public attributes.

## §10 How this sets up Stage 2

Stage 2 replaces `hand_policy()` / `doors_hand_policy()` with a **CFG-driven production emitter** that the existing
[`LiftedDerivationGame`](../../../src/alphazeropp/synthesis/lifted_derivation.py) (uniform-prior MCTS) can drive, scored
by the leaf evaluator's episodic rollout. The dispatch primitive built here — `interpret(policy, state, goal, objects,
legal_actions, *, trace=False) -> GroundAction | None` — is what the leaf evaluator calls once per env step, and the env
contract `{get_state_atoms, get_goal_atoms, get_objects_by_type, legal_actions, step, reset, is_solved}` is exactly what
a grammar's productions and a leaf evaluator's rollout consume. `gripper_lite_signature()` and `doors_pddl_signature()`
(both in [lifted_grammar.py](../../../src/alphazeropp/synthesis/lifted_grammar.py)) are the `DomainSignature`s the
derivation game is parametrized by. The Gripper-lite and Doors-PDDL relational envs both graduate from "Stage-1 test
rig" to "production domain" the moment a leaf evaluator wires them up.
